# Measuring what reaches the model

*How much of what an issue needs does each way of choosing files deliver within a token
budget, and how sure can we be? A lab report on Repo2Txt's `select`, 15–16 November 2026. It
follows [the tuning report of 14 November](report-2026-11-14.md), whose numbers it rechecks.*

## Summary

- **A pre-registered test on unseen work.** On 254 issues from 21 repositories that no method
  had been run or tuned on, the model selector delivers more of the needed files than the
  lexical default at 32,000 tokens with each of three models: +0.19 with Claude Haiku 5.5,
  +0.15 with Claude Sonnet 5.5 and +0.20 with DeepSeek flash. The lexical default's three
  signals add +0.16 over plain BM25. The tests were written down before the runs; every
  interval lies above zero.
- **The smallest model is the right one.** Haiku matches DeepSeek and beats Sonnet on recall,
  at a sixth and a fourteenth of their cost. Sonnet chooses fewer files and misses more.
- **It transfers.** On Agent Retrieval Bench, a benchmark built differently and scored by its
  own evaluator, the model selector reaches a Budgeted Context Yield of 0.63 at 8,000 tokens,
  against 0.38 for the benchmark's repo map, 0.37 for its best embedding model and 0.53 for the
  best community result. The lexical default does not transfer: one of its signals is wrong for
  that benchmark's test-finding task.
- **What "needed" means changes the numbers, not the order.** Three definitions (annotated by
  people, edited by the fix, read by successful agents) disagree by up to half, and every
  method's score moves with them; the order of the methods does not.
- **The earlier harness had faults, now fixed and tested.** None changed a conclusion; one
  inflated a line-range claim, which is restated here.

## The question, and what "needed" means

A coding agent works from what fits in its context. Repo2Txt's `select` chooses files for a
request within a budget, either lexically or by asking a small model. The question is how much
of what an issue needs arrives.

ContextBench (Li et al., 2026) supplies, for each issue, the code people traced out from the
fix and checked was enough for a model to write one. That is one sufficient set, not the only
one. Two more definitions were added to test whether conclusions depend on it:

- **Edited**: the lines the fix changed, read from its patch. On ContextBench's full release,
  the annotated context omits at least one file the fix edited in 21% of tasks, nearly all from
  its Multi-SWE-bench source.
- **Explored**: SWE-Explore's lines that at least two successful agent runs read (Zhang et al.,
  2026), available for a minority of issues.

On the fresh held-out set, a perfect choice of the annotated files delivers 0.80 of the edited
files and 0.60 of the explored ones; a perfect choice of the edited files delivers 0.60 of the
annotated ones. "Needed" is a family of answers.

## The harness, rebuilt

An audit of the first harness found it sound in coverage and budgets, and found problems a
reviewer would rightly object to. Each is fixed and has a test:

- **Line ranges were scored on a different footing from whole files.** A file counted as
  delivered if any one of its lines was shown, and that score sat in the same table as a
  whole-file ceiling it could exceed. Line methods are now scored only by line recall, in their
  own table.
- **Overlapping gold spans counted twice,** and some spans ran past the end of their file,
  which let line precision exceed 1. Spans are merged and clipped.
- **The line ceiling was costed differently** from the line method. Both now use the library's
  own packing cost.
- **The data was not pinned.** ContextBench is now read at a fixed revision with recorded
  hashes and a committed task manifest. The pinned data matched what earlier results used,
  byte for byte.
- **Nothing recorded which code produced a row.** Every row now carries a hash of the code
  (including uncommitted changes), the dataset revision, the model, effort and prompt.
- **Empty selections scored zero precision,** pulling averages down. They now have none.

The bench now calls `repo_select` itself, so it measures what the command delivers, and a
self-check asserts on real tasks that oracles reach their ceilings, budgets hold and runs
reproduce. One statistics module, tested against scipy, computes every interval by resampling
whole repositories.

## How much runs vary

Four held-out runs of the same configuration gave mean recall 0.748, 0.744, 0.741 and 0.741 at
32k. 91% of the variance lies between issues and 9% between runs, so one run settles an average
and more repositories, not more runs, would narrow its interval. A quarter of single issues
changed between runs: an example drawn from one issue needs that caveat.

## Outside measurements

**Agent Retrieval Bench** (Qin and Xie, 2026) asks for the files an agent needs next from four
kinds of workflow signal. Its samples, corpus and evaluator were used unmodified, at the hashes
published with its community results.

| Ranking (345 samples) | Recall@5 | Recall@20 | MRR | BCY@8k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model selector, Haiku 5.5 | 0.60 | 0.66 | 0.41 | 0.63 |
| Model selector, DeepSeek flash | 0.58 | 0.65 | 0.42 | 0.61 |
| Model selector, Sonnet 5.5 | 0.53 | 0.57 | 0.42 | 0.53 |
| Oko 0.9.1, community result | 0.49 | 0.70 | 0.40 | 0.53 |
| RepoMap, published | 0.31 | 0.63 | 0.22 | 0.38 |
| Qwen3-Embedding-8B, published | 0.36 | 0.70 | 0.23 | 0.37 |
| Lexical default | 0.22 | 0.43 | 0.14 | 0.27 |
| Plain BM25 | 0.21 | 0.50 | 0.18 | 0.24 |

Each model selector's dev and held-out halves agree. The lexical default's signals help where
a failing trace names files (Recall@20 0.89 against 0.71 for plain BM25) and fail where the job
is to find tests (0.07 against 0.34), because one signal ranks tests last. Reading the model
selector's misses showed the same lean: on test-finding it chose the code under change, and its
closing message mentioned a related test it had left out for room.

**Aider's repo map**, run with aider's own mention rules and ranking on the held-out split,
delivers 0.14 of the needed files at 32k, below plain BM25 (0.34). It is built to summarise the
code around files a user has already added, and only recognises a file mention when the path
stands as its own word; this measures it in a role it was not built for. A first version of the
adapter left named files out of the chat, which aider would not do; it was corrected before the
number was recorded.

## Calling models natively

The earlier results used Anthropic's OpenAI-compatible endpoint, which its documentation calls
unsuitable for production: it ignores effort, rejects adaptive thinking and supports no prompt
caching. Each model is now called through its provider's own API, with effort set explicitly
(Haiku and Sonnet medium, DeepSeek high, its lowest honoured level), reasoning passed back
between rounds and prompt caching on.

That exposed a fault in the selector: its system prompt restated the current selection, so the
start of every request changed each round and no cache could reuse it. Fixing it cost recall on
the tuning tasks (0.636 against 0.677 through the same route; −0.041, −0.073 to −0.014); the
route itself made no difference (+0.014, −0.013 to +0.046). Ending each tool reply with the
selected paths, where the model reads next, recovered most of the loss (0.661; the remaining
gap is −0.016, −0.042 to +0.007) while keeping the opening stable. With caching, 55–67% of input
was read from cache and Haiku's cost per selection fell by 40%.

A prompt written to the middle ground of current Anthropic, OpenAI and DeepSeek guidance (goal,
reason and stop condition; conditional wording; tool descriptions that say when to use each)
was tested on two models and not adopted: on Haiku it cost 0.040 recall (0.012 to 0.072) for
higher precision, and on DeepSeek it made no measurable difference.

## The pre-registered run

The configuration was frozen and the tests written down
([bench/preregistration-2026-11-15.md](../bench/preregistration-2026-11-15.md)) before any run on
the fresh held-out set: the 255 tasks of ContextBench's full release outside the verified subset
on the held-out side of the repository split, from 21 repositories. One task proved to have no
issue text and was skipped; two repositories failed to download once and were retried. Both are
recorded as deviations.

| Recall (precision) | 8k | 32k | 128k |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smallest files first | 0.01 | 0.04 | 0.12 |
| Plain BM25 | 0.09 (0.08) | 0.24 (0.10) | 0.44 (0.07) |
| `select` | 0.19 (0.12) | 0.40 (0.14) | 0.56 (0.09) |
| Model selector, Haiku 5.5 | 0.34 (0.42) | 0.59 (0.41) | |
| Model selector, Sonnet 5.5 | 0.33 (0.56) | 0.55 (0.58) | |
| Model selector, DeepSeek flash | 0.35 (0.35) | 0.60 (0.27) | |
| Ceiling: the needed files | 0.61 | 0.89 | 0.99 |

| Pre-registered test, 32k | Difference (95% interval) | Better / worse issues |
|---|---|---|
| Haiku selector − `select` | +0.186 (+0.144 to +0.238) | 132 / 16 |
| Sonnet selector − `select` | +0.148 (+0.111 to +0.200) | 116 / 23 |
| DeepSeek selector − `select` | +0.196 (+0.150 to +0.235) | 129 / 19 |
| `select` − plain BM25 | +0.156 (+0.096 to +0.218) | 108 / 12 |

Leaving out any one repository moves each difference by at most 0.02. Against the edited and
explored definitions the order holds.

The fresh set is harder than the earlier held-out set for every method (`select` 0.40 against
0.55, Haiku 0.59 against 0.75): its issues are spread differently. That the gaps hold across
both sets matters more than the levels.

**The models.** Haiku and DeepSeek do not differ on recall (+0.010, −0.034 to +0.040); Sonnet
trails Haiku by 0.038 (0.013 to 0.064). For 255 selections at 32k, Haiku cost $0.47, DeepSeek
$3.00 and Sonnet $6.72. Sonnet is the most precise (0.58) and picks the fewest files; DeepSeek
uses about four rounds of tool calls to Haiku's 2.7, writes five times the output and takes
four times as long. For choosing what to read, more model did not help.

**Line ranges.** At 8k, Haiku choosing line ranges delivers 0.48 of the needed lines against
0.38 choosing whole files (+0.095, +0.038 to +0.166; better on 107 issues, worse on 31). The
ceiling for whole files is 0.53 and for the lines themselves 0.92, so most of that room remains.
(The earlier report's "file recall from 0.47 to 0.70" for line ranges counted a file as found
when any one of its lines was shown, and is withdrawn.)

## What could overturn this

- **One annotation family.** Two of the three definitions come from patches and annotations of
  the same issues; SWE-Explore covers few of them. A downstream test (does a model fix the
  issue from what was delivered) was not run.
- **Memory of the fix.** Whether the models had seen these repositories and their fixes was not
  measured. The lexical rows are unaffected.
- **21 repositories.** Intervals are wide, and five repositories carry many of the tasks. The
  repository-weighted means and leave-one-out ranges are reported for that reason.
- **The structure trade.** The stable prefix that makes caching work may still cost about two
  points of recall on the tuning tasks; the interval includes zero.
- **One run per configuration** on the fresh set. Repeats elsewhere put the spread of a mean at
  0.003; single issues vary far more.
- **Token counts** are o200k_base; each provider's tokeniser differs.

## Spend

Model calls across both reports came to about $55: the tuning work about $11 at Haiku's actual
price (the first report assumed ten times that and is corrected here), and about $43 for this
one (the fresh-set runs $21.74, Agent Retrieval Bench at most $16, pilots and experiments about
$5).
